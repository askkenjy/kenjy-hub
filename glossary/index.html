Terms

Agent

An AI setup with a goal and steps (not just one chat reply). It can repeat a workflow, like “turn this brief into three listings.”

AI (Artificial Intelligence)

Software that can do tasks that usually need human-like language or judgment — writing, summarizing, brainstorming — when you give clear instructions.

API

A way for apps to talk to each other. You usually won’t touch this as a beginner; tools use it behind the scenes.

Artifact

The file or output you can keep, send, or sell: a doc, pitch, caption set, listing, SOP. If it isn’t an artifact, it’s still practice.

Chatbot

A chat interface on top of a model. The box you type into. The model is the engine; the chatbot is the steering wheel.

ChatGPT

OpenAI’s popular AI chatbot product. One place beginners start conversations with a language model.

Claude

Anthropic’s AI chatbot/assistant product. Another option for writing, reasoning, and long documents.

Club (AI Money Club)

Weekly beginner practice + accountability on the curriculum path. Soft upsell after Free Pack — not a magic income guarantee.

Connector

A bridge from the AI to your real accounts (Drive, email, store) so work can move in and out without endless copy-paste.

Context window

How much text the model can “keep in mind” at once (your prompt + prior messages + pasted docs). If you overload it, quality drops.

Custom GPT / Project

A saved assistant with instructions and files so you don’t re-explain yourself every time.

Embedding / vector search (light)

A way to find related chunks of your docs by meaning. Advanced; skip until you need a knowledge base.

Fine-tuning

Extra training that specializes a model on a narrower set of examples. Most beginners don’t need this — good prompts come first.

Free Pack

Kenjy’s free starter checklist/map. Enter via /enter/ (email gate) then /free/.

Grok

xAI’s AI assistant. Another chatbot option in the same beginner toolkit category (chat → clear outcomes).

Guardrails

Limits that keep AI use safe and on-brand (what you won’t generate, what you always verify).

Hallucination

When the AI sounds confident but is wrong or made something up. Always check facts for money, legal, health, or client work.

Hallucination check

Your habit of verifying important claims before you post or send to a client.

LLM (Large Language Model)

The big text-prediction system behind many chatbots. It predicts useful next words from patterns in data — it doesn’t “know” like a person.

Model

The underlying AI brain (the LLM or similar). Different products may use different models.

Multimodal

AI that can work with more than text (e.g. images + text). Handy later; not required for Month 1.

Offer

What you sell: who it’s for + the outcome + when it’s delivered (+ price). Tools stay in the kitchen.

Output

Whatever the AI returns: text, a list, a draft, code, a plan. Your job is to shape outputs into artifacts.

Plugin

An add-on inside an AI app that adds a capability (browse, format, niche tools). Optional — only add one if it saves real delivery time.

Prompt

The instructions you type (or paste). Clear prompts = clearer paid work. Vague prompts = rework.

Prompt engineering

The skill of writing better prompts on purpose: role, task, constraints, examples, format.

System prompt

Hidden or preset instructions that steer the assistant’s behavior before your message. Custom GPTs/projects often use these.

Temperature

A setting that controls how “creative vs careful” outputs feel. Higher = more variety; lower = more steady and predictable.

Token

A chunk of text the model reads/writes (roughly pieces of words). Limits and pricing are often counted in tokens.

Training data

The text/examples used to teach a model before you use it. You don’t upload this for normal chatting.
